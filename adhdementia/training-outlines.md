# ADHDementia training: course skeletons, accreditation and platform notes

Working document for Kerry. The interactive online course is built at `course.html`
with draft content ready to be replaced with final text. The two live formats below
are structured around the same six modules so that all three share one accreditable
curriculum, one set of learning outcomes and one certificate framework.

---

## The shared curriculum (all three formats)

Six modules, one spine:

1. **The Lost Generation.** Why ADHD assessment did not exist for this generation,
   who was missed (especially women with internalised presentations), and what the
   traits were called instead.
2. **ADHD Meets Dementia.** The emerging evidence (around three times the dementia
   risk, highest when untreated), why the care home cohort is the untreated cohort,
   and the one question that changes everything: was this trait lifelong?
3. **Recognising the Traits.** The nine domains of the ADHDementia framework, with
   care home examples for each: object permanence and the tidied room, time
   blindness and waiting paralysis, hyperfocus, masking.
4. **Reframing Behaviour.** Sensory distress behind "challenging behaviour", the
   need for why behind "non-compliance", rejection sensitive dysphoria behind
   "disproportionate" reactions.
5. **Practical Adjustments.** Environment, communication, routines, and care plan
   language: what to write instead of "refuses" and "difficult".
6. **Screening, Dignity and Next Steps.** What the screening tool does and does not
   do, asking the person rather than about them, and feeding profiles into care plans.

Learning outcomes (used on all certificates and accreditation paperwork):

- Explain why many older adults have ADHD that was never diagnosed
- Recognise common ADHD traits and how they present in dementia care
- Reframe "challenging behaviour" as possible unmet neurodivergent need
- Apply practical adjustments in their own role
- Understand how screening supports person-centred care planning

## Format 1: Online course (built, about 1 hour)

Self-paced and interactive, aimed at induction programmes. Already implemented at
`course.html`:

- Welcome screen with learning outcomes and learner name capture (for the certificate)
- Six modules that unlock in order, each with reading blocks, key points and
  scenario-based knowledge checks with instant feedback
- Progress saved on the learner's device, resume supported
- Final assessment: 10 scenario questions, pass mark 8/10, unlimited retakes
- Printable certificate with learner name, date and certificate ID

Timings: modules 8 + 8 + 12 + 12 + 10 + 5 minutes, assessment about 8 minutes.

## Format 2: Online webinar (2 hours, live, morning)

Same six modules compressed, with live interaction where the online course has
knowledge checks:

| Time | Session |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Welcome, who is in the room, one poll: "How many of your residents might have ADHD?" |
| 0:10 | Modules 1 and 2: the lost generation and the evidence |
| 0:35 | Module 3: the nine traits, with live "spot the trait" scenarios in chat |
| 1:00 | Short break |
| 1:05 | Module 4: reframing behaviour, case discussion in breakout or chat |
| 1:30 | Module 5: practical adjustments, "what could you change on your next shift?" |
| 1:50 | Module 6, questions, and the screening tool demonstration |
| 2:00 | Close. Certificates emailed after attendance is confirmed |

## Format 3: On-site training day (full day)

The webinar content taught in depth, plus everything only possible in the building:

| Time | Session |
|---|---|
| 9:30 | Welcome and introductions. The lost generation and the evidence (Modules 1 and 2) |
| 10:45 | Break |
| 11:00 | The nine traits in depth, with residents' (anonymised) screening profiles as live case studies (Module 3) |
| 12:30 | Lunch |
| 13:15 | Environment walk-through: dining room, lounges, corridors, a resident room (with permission), identifying sensory pressure points |
| 14:15 | Reframing behaviour and the care plan language workshop: rewriting real (anonymised) entries (Modules 4 and 5) |
| 15:30 | Break |
| 15:45 | Screening with dignity, embedding it in practice, action planning per role (Module 6) |
| 16:30 | Close, evaluation forms, certificates |

The environment walk-through and care plan workshop are the on-site day's unique
value, and the reason it commands the premium price.

## Accreditation route (recommended order)

1. **CPD Certification Service (start here).** The pragmatic first step used by most
   independent training providers. They assess the course structure, learning
   outcomes and assessment, and once accredited the certificates can carry "CPD
   Certified" and CPD hours. Expect an annual provider fee plus a per-course
   assessment fee (budget roughly GBP 1,000 to 2,000 for year one). Everything they
   ask for already exists: stated learning outcomes, structured modules, assessment
   with a pass mark, certificates, and (for live formats) evaluation forms.
2. **Skills for Care.** The sector's own endorsement scheme for adult social care
   learning providers. A higher bar and a longer process, but it is the mark care
   home managers and commissioners recognise most, and it materially helps when
   selling to groups. Apply once the course has run with real learners and evaluation
   evidence has accumulated.
3. **Alignment, not accreditation:** describe the course as complementing the Oliver
   McGowan Mandatory Training (the statutory baseline for autism and learning
   disability), never as replacing it. The live formats already meet two McGowan
   themes that buyers care about: live interactive delivery, and design informed by
   lived experience of neurodivergence.

What accreditors will ask for (checklist):

- [ ] Named author with relevant credentials (doctoral researcher, lived experience, care experience)
- [ ] Learning outcomes per course (done, above)
- [ ] Module structure with timings (done, above)
- [ ] Assessment method and pass mark (done: 10 questions, 80%)
- [ ] Certificate template with learner name, date, hours, unique ID (done, in `course.html`)
- [ ] Learner evaluation mechanism (add a short feedback form at course end)
- [ ] Review cycle commitment (e.g. content reviewed annually against new evidence)
- [ ] References list for the evidence claims (add once the research document arrives)

## Platform and format strategy for the e-learning course

**Phase 1 (now): sell it from adhdementia.com.** The course is a self-contained web
page in the site's own design, gated behind the subscription or sold per seat.
No platform fees, no revenue share, full control, and certificates built in.

**Phase 2 (to sell via eLFY, My Learning Hippo and other care sector LMS
platforms): SCORM.** Care sector learning management systems buy courses as SCORM
packages (SCORM 1.2 remains the universal safe choice; some also take xAPI).
Two routes:

- *Wrap the existing course.* The current HTML course can be packaged as a SCORM
  module with a thin wrapper that reports completion and score to the host LMS.
  Cheapest route, keeps one set of content, and can be done when the first buyer
  asks for it.
- *Rebuild in an authoring tool.* Articulate Rise 360 (about GBP 1,100 per year) or
  iSpring Suite (about GBP 600 per year) export SCORM natively and are what
  commercial course buyers expect to see. Worth doing when a distribution deal is
  close, not before.

**Recommendation:** stay with the owned web version until accreditation is secured
and a distributor shows real interest, then produce the SCORM build as part of that
deal. Distributors take a large revenue share, so direct sales at full margin are
the better business while the audience is being built anyway.

**Certificates for the live formats:** issue from the same template as the online
course (name, format, date, hours, ID), sent as PDF after the session.
